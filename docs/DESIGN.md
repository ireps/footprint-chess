# Design

## Audience

Children around six years old. This includes children who find left and right, reading, or changes of viewpoint difficult, and children with short attention spans.

## Principles

1. **Show direction, never name it.** The app never says left, right or a square name like e4 (the hand-print levels, which teach left and right on purpose, are the one exception). Tapping a piece marks every square it can reach with footprints.
2. **Fixed viewpoint.** The board never rotates. The child's side is always at the bottom.
3. **Real chess terms, everywhere, in every language.** Every piece is always named and spoken of by its real chess name (rook, bishop, queen, king, knight, pawn) and every capture is called a capture. The board's two edges are "your side" (row 7, the child's home edge) and "the other side" (row 0, the opponent's edge). Voice lines use them for direction ("toward the other side"; Telugu అవతలి వైపు) and never say left or right. The Telugu names are king రాజు, queen మంత్రి, rook ఏనుగు, bishop ఒంటె, knight గుర్రం, pawn భటుడు, and a chess piece generally is పావు. The robot theme is decoration, not vocabulary: no robot name (or word for robot, junk or bump) is ever said or written to the child, in any language.
4. **Characters whose movement matches the rule.** The robot theme is a costume over the real piece: every robot is always shown with a small badge of the real piece's silhouette, and always named with its real chess name (see the terminology rule above), never the robot's own nickname.

   | Piece  | Movement animation | Robot look |
   |--------|--------------------|------------|
   | Rook   | Glides along straight lines | Battlements |
   | Bishop | Swooshes along diagonals | Mitre |
   | Queen  | Glides either way and leaves sparkles | Crown with sparkles |
   | King   | Shuffles one square | Crown with a cross |
   | Knight | Two hops: two squares, then one to the side | Horse head |
   | Pawn   | Marches forward, captures diagonally | Round head |

   Opponent pawns (the capture rounds' targets) are the same pawn robot in dark colours, standing in for "the other side"; they are still always called pawns.

5. **Short rounds.** Each round lasts about 60 to 90 seconds and has one goal. Feedback arrives within about 100 ms of a tap.
6. **No fail states.** On a wrong tap the piece wiggles and the footprints pulse. After 5 seconds without a tap, a hint plays. An opponent pawn that can no longer be captured moves to a square where it can.
7. **Everything is open.** No level is locked.
8. **Calm by default.** Motion responds to the child's actions. Nothing loops or flashes while the child is thinking. The system reduced-motion setting is respected: it removes motion (animations finish at once, effects are skipped) but never changes pacing, so lesson pauses, the Meet card's minimum time, the opponent's thinking pause and similar timings stay the same. Calm mode (in the grown-ups' corner) goes further: no board bump, smaller capture bursts, no full-screen confetti and softer sound effects.
9. **Almost no text.** Instructions are short voice lines with pictures and animation.
10. **Original artwork only.** Themes do not use characters or artwork from existing books, films, games or brands.
11. **Always clear who is in control.** While the app is showing something, the board has a purple ring and a "Watch" badge (eye icon; Telugu చూడు), and taps on the board only wiggle the badge. When control passes to the child, the ring turns green and the badge pops to "Your turn!" (hand icon; Telugu నీ వంతు!) with a bright chime, followed by the "Now you try!" line. Rounds are always in the "Your turn" state; cards and the home screen show neither. Every future activity uses the same two signals.

## Flow

Every activity states its goal before it starts, in voice and in a picture, and has a clear ending.

- **Home.** Six piece cards (robot art, a real-piece badge, the name in the current language). Nothing is ever locked; the suggested card (the first piece the current child has not yet played, rook at first) glows with a play badge, but any card can be tapped; a piece that has been played has a small green tick. Top-left: the child's own picture, which opens Who's playing. Top-right: the sticker book, theme, language, sound, and a "?" link to [help.html](../help.html) for grown-ups (held for two seconds, it opens the grown-ups' corner). Below the games, a shelf of up to four stickers.
- **Meet.** The first time a piece is chosen, a card shows the real piece shape next to the robot character and says its name ("This is the rook."). Continues on a tap, or on its own once the line has finished and a couple of seconds have passed.
- **Lesson.** The piece's "watch, then do" lesson (see below), followed by a short lesson on capturing with that piece, the first time each is needed.
- **Mission.** Before every round, a card states the goal out loud and in pictures ("Capture all three pawns!") with three pawn pictures and a play button. Shown every time, even on a repeat round.
- **Round.** The child captures three opponent pawns with the chosen piece. A captured pawn fills a slot in the side panel's goal.
- **Won.** A short cheer and confetti, then a card: play the same piece again, try the next piece, or go home.
- **Games.** One Games button on Home opens the Games screen (see "Games" below).

## Lessons

Each lesson teaches one idea as "watch, then do" on the real board.

- **Watch (10 to 18 seconds).** A short script of voice lines, moves, footprints, glowing squares and edge pulses. A ghost hand shows where to tap. Taps on the board are ignored during this part.
- **Do.** The child repeats it on the same position, following the ghost hand: tap the piece, then the footprint the hand rests on. Any legal move counts unless the lesson needs a specific square (every capturing lesson). Wrong taps make the piece wiggle and the footprints pulse, with no voice. After 5 seconds idle, a short voice hint plays and the hand taps once.
- **Replay and Skip.** Replay plays the current piece's lesson again, from the Meet card. Skip appears only during a Meet card or a lesson, and goes straight to that piece's Mission card.

| Lesson | Idea | When it plays |
|--------|------|---------------|
| Hello | Tap your piece, footprints appear, tap a footprint | The first piece ever chosen, before its own lesson |
| Rook | Straight lines, as far as it likes | The first time the rook is chosen |
| Bishop | Slanted lines, always on its own colour | The first time the bishop is chosen |
| Queen | Straight and slanted together | The first time the queen is chosen |
| King | One step, any way | The first time the king is chosen |
| Knight | Two squares, then one to the side; jumps over things | The first time the knight is chosen |
| Pawn | Marches toward the other side; the first step can be two | The first time the pawn is chosen |
| Capture: rook / bishop / queen / king / knight | Move onto an opponent pawn's square to capture it | Once per child, right after the first non-pawn piece's own lesson (a later piece goes straight to its Mission card) |
| Pawn capture | A pawn captures on the slant, one step ahead, never straight ahead | Right after the pawn's own lesson, the first time |

The capture idea is taught once per child, with the first non-pawn piece they meet: later pieces go straight from their own lesson to the Mission card. The pawn's slant capture is different, so it always gets its own lesson the first time. Each capture lesson shows the capture once, then the child practises it.

"The first time" means the first time for the current child: which lessons have been seen is saved for each child (see "Profiles, stickers and the grown-ups' corner").

### Voice

The voice lines are listed in [VOICE-SCRIPT.md](VOICE-SCRIPT.md), in English and Telugu. English is the default language; Telugu is chosen with `?lang=te` in the address bar, and a language button (on the home screen and in the side panel) switches at any time without losing a line already playing. The languages are listed in one registry, `js/langs.js` (id, names, button glyph, device-voice prefix and preferences, fallback language, Azure voice, the wording of "<team>'s turn"); every part of the app that depends on the set of languages reads it, so adding a language is mostly a data change (see [LANGUAGES.md](LANGUAGES.md)). With exactly two languages the button switches to the other one and shows that language's glyph; with three or more it opens a small language row like the theme row, one round button per language showing its glyph, with the current one ringed. `document.documentElement.lang` stays `en`, since the app's own labels are all English; only the spoken lines change.

For the current language, a line plays from a recorded clip if one exists, otherwise through the device's speech synthesis, otherwise, for a language with a fallback (Telugu, whose fallback is English), the same two steps again in the fallback language, otherwise silently with its estimated length so the pacing stays the same. The default language never falls back to another language. Device speech is deliberately soft: a slightly slow rate and a slightly raised pitch, with a voice chosen to sound less flat than the browser default where one is available. The sound button turns voice off together with the sound effects.

**Narration interrupts, announcements queue.** `FC.voice.say(id, done)` is for lesson narration and flow steps: it stops whatever is playing, drops the queue, and the interrupted line's `done` is never called. `FC.voice.sayAfter(id, done)` is for announcements that must not cut one another off ("A golden pawn!" then "You got a sticker!", the team name then the game's mission line, "The knight is getting tired!", "Your piece is back!", "Their turn." / "Your turn!", a sticker line then the win line). With nothing playing it behaves like `say`; otherwise the line waits in a queue of at most four and plays when the current line has finished and its `done` has run. A later `say` or `stop` drops the queue without calling its callbacks. When the queue is full the oldest item without a callback is dropped; an item whose callback drives the flow (a win line that then opens the Won card) is never dropped.

Voice clips are generated by the owner from the wording in [VOICE-SCRIPT.md](VOICE-SCRIPT.md), currently with their own local text-to-speech script; [tools/make-voice.js](../tools/make-voice.js) (Azure neural text-to-speech) is an alternative. See VOICE-SCRIPT.md for both workflows and for the recording spec that also applies to a parent's own recordings.

## Games

The Games button on Home opens the Games screen, a full screen like Home with a Home button and three rows of game cards. Each row starts with a picture instead of a word: capture games (an opponent pawn in a burst), reach-the-other-side games (a finish flag over a pawn) and thinking games (footprints and a question mark). Every card can be tapped; the first game not yet won in the current theme glows, and a won game has a green tick. The list of games, their rows, lines and whether they are played in teams is one registry, `js/game-list.js`; the rules are in `js/games.js` (board games) and `js/quiz.js` (the quiz), both without DOM and tested.

Nine short games, three per row. Four are against a gentle opponent (`js/games.js`, tested for "the child always finishes"): **Catch the knight** (the last piece chosen on Home, not the pawn, chases a knight that hops away after every move and tires after six of the child's moves), **Pawn race** (three pawns each; the first to reach the other side wins; the opponent stays at least one row short of the child's side and never leaves the child with fewer than two pawns) **Little battle** (the rook plus up to two other pieces whose lessons the child has seen clear four opponent pawns; a captured piece returns on the child's back row) and **Stop the pawns** (the last piece chosen on Home, not the bishop, which could never reach a pawn stopped on the other colour; three opponent pawns march one step a turn toward the child's side, never capture and stop at row 6; a pawn with a piece straight in front of it cannot move, which the tip shows; the child captures all three). Five are solo, with no opponent turns and no teams: **Knight hop** (the knight reaches the other side, hopping over or capturing three still pawns), **Find the way** (one piece, a new one each game from rook, bishop, queen and king, reaches the other side; the child's own pawns stand in the way, and only the one piece moves, so the child learns that a piece cannot pass or capture its own team; the other side is never one move away), **Keep the king safe** (the king walks to the other side past a still opponent rook and bishop; his footprints only show squares neither could capture on, the squares he may not step to glow red when he is selected, and tapping one says "Not there! That square is not safe for the king." He may capture a guard that the other does not protect. This is the idea behind check, taught without the word), **Capture chain** (one piece, the last one chosen on Home at first and the next one each new chain, and four still pawns placed so that each capture lands one move from the next pawn; at the start only the first pawn can be captured; after 5 idle seconds the next pawn of the chain glows) and **Whose footprints?** (see "Quiz" below). In Catch the knight, Capture chain, Stop the pawns and the three reach-the-other-side solo games, 5 idle seconds with nothing selected make a hint square glow: the next pawn of the chain, the first step toward the nearest pawn, or the next step of a shortest way to the other side (`FC.games.hint`). None has a score, lives or a timer. Every game states its goal on a Mission card, in voice and in a picture, and ends on a Won card (again, the next game, the game's tip, home).

**Quiz.** "Whose footprints?" asks five questions, one piece each (no piece twice). Footprints appear around an empty glowing square, all in one grey so their colour does not give the answer away, and three of the child's pieces stand on row 7. The voice asks "Whose footprints are these?" (Watch), then it is the child's turn. The right piece steps onto the square, its footprints light up in its own colour and its rule line is said (for example "Our rook always moves in straight lines."). A wrong piece wiggles and shows its own footprints from the same square while "Its footprints look different. Try again!" plays; then the question's footprints return. After two wrong taps on one question the ghost hand rests on the answer. The two other choices are always the pieces most often mixed up with the answer (rook: queen, king; bishop: queen, pawn; queen: rook, bishop; king: queen, pawn; knight: king, bishop; pawn: king, rook), and never a piece that would make the same footprints. Footprints on row 7 are left out, since the choices stand there.

**Watch how to play.** Every game has a short scene on the board showing how to play it well (purple ring, "Watch how to play!", Skip ends it). It plays after the Mission card the first time each child plays that game (saved as a seen `how-<game>`), then the game starts; the light bulb on the Mission card and on the Won card plays it again at any time. The scenes: for Catch the knight, a worked example of catching a piece that moves differently from yours ("The knight can hop to these squares." with the knight's footprints; the rook moves so its footprints cover some of those squares, which glow, "Stand where your footprints cover its hops."; the knight hops onto one, "It landed on your footprints. Capture it!", and the rook captures it); a pawn's two-square first step (Race); two pieces each showing their own footprints (Battle); a capture that lands one move from the next pawn (Chain); the knight hopping to the footprints nearest the other side (Knight hop); the rook's footprints stopping at its own pawn and the rook going round (Find the way); the rook standing in front of a pawn so it cannot march, then capturing it (Stop the pawns); the king's footprints beside the red squares a rook watches (Keep the king safe); and the rook, bishop and queen patterns in turn (Quiz). In Catch the knight the idle hint glows the knight when it can be captured, otherwise the move that puts the most of its next hops on the piece's footprints.

**Tips after a game.** After the win line, the board can show a short rule tip (purple ring, "Here is a little tip!", Skip ends it), then the Won card. If the child made two or more wrong taps with one piece type in the game (a tap on a square the selected piece cannot reach; in the quiz, a wrong answer when that piece was the answer), that piece stands in the middle of the board with its footprints, its rule line plays and it walks to the footprint nearest the other side. Otherwise no tip plays.

Flow: game card, the "Taking turns" lesson (the first team game each child plays; Skip is available), the Team card (every time a team game is started from the Games screen; the team the child chose last time in this theme is ringed, and either can be tapped), the Mission card, the game, a tip when one is due, the Won card. Solo games go straight to the Mission card. "Play again" and the next game on the Won card skip the Team card once a team is chosen. The game's starting position is set up behind the Mission card, and the side panel shows the game's own goal picture instead of the capture round's three pawn slots.

**Turn-taking.** The "Taking turns" lesson uses two extra script steps: `{ turn }` lights one team bar and plays a soft tick (no voice; the next spoken line narrates, and the badge stays "Watch"), and `{ foeMove }` moves the opponent's pawn. Its first practice task has a `reply`: after the child's move the player shows the opponent's turn (badge "<team>'s turn", top bar lit, "Their turn."), waits, moves the opponent, then hands control back (chime, green ring, bottom bar lit, "Your turn!"). In games the opponent's turn follows the same pattern, with a 0.6 to 0.9 second pause before it moves; "Their turn." is spoken on every other opponent turn only, "Your turn!" every time.

**Teams.** Each theme has two teams (`js/themes.js` TEAMS): Humanoids and Androids, White and Black, Astronauts and Cosmonauts, Theropods and Sauropods, Buccaneers and Corsairs. Team a is the "White" side and moves first. The child picks either team; the chosen team always plays from the bottom (the board never rotates), so picking team b means the opponent moves first. The edge strips show a team bar (a pawn and the name), top for the other team and bottom for the child's, the active one lit and the other dimmed to 45% opacity, during games and the "Taking turns" lesson only. In Classic, playing Black swaps the two colour sets (the child's pieces black, the opponent's ivory); every other theme keeps the child's per-type colours whichever team is picked.

**Juice.** Every capture, in games and capture rounds, gives a burst of 8 to 12 particles at the square, a small board bump, a hop of the capturing piece, and a chime one semitone higher for each capture in a row (at most six steps; the run resets on a move that captures nothing and when a round or game ends).

**Jar and stickers.** Each captured pawn (not the knight) fills a jar in the panel's mission box, a picture with no numbers. A full jar (10) empties into a sticker: a spoken "You got a sticker!" and a round gold-rimmed badge. About one round or game in five has one golden pawn; capturing it is an instant sticker. The sticker is of the piece the child was playing (the chasing piece in Catch, the piece in Capture chain, Find the way and Stop the pawns, the knight in Knight hop, the king in Keep the king safe, the pawn in Pawn race, the rook in Little battle, the round's piece in a capture round); a golden pawn gives a golden pawn sticker. Winning three different games in a theme for the first time gives that theme's golden king, with the same pop and line. Stickers are saved for each child and shown in the sticker book; the four most recent sit on a shelf on Home.

**Break reminder.** Active play time (lessons, rounds and games; not time on Home) is counted. After the chosen length (15 minutes at first; 10, 15, 20 or 30 in the grown-ups' corner, which can also switch the reminder off), the next Won card is replaced by a calm Break card (a moon, "Great playing! Time for a little break?", keep playing or home). Keep playing resets the timer, as does Home. `?break=off` disables it for a visit. Test hooks: `?breakmins=<minutes>` overrides the length (it never turns the reminder on) and `?golden=1` forces a golden pawn; both are described in README.md and change nothing else.

## Themes

Five themes: Robots (the default), Classic, Space, Dinosaurs and Pirate (`js/themes.js`). Every theme keeps the same real-piece silhouettes, real chess names and badges (principle 3, above); only the piece artwork and the board/panel colours change. Chosen with `?theme=<id>` in the address bar, exactly like `?lang=`, or with a palette button on the home screen; an unrecognised id falls back to Robots. A valid `?theme=` wins at load; otherwise the current child's saved theme is used, and a theme (or language) the child picks is saved for that child.

The world (board, frame, edge strips, background) and the other side's pieces get natural colours for each theme: one dark "other side" piece colour, and, apart from Classic, one accent colour for costume details (Pirate: also a gold, a stripe and a trouser colour, for its crew's shirts, sashes and trousers). The child's own pieces keep the same per-type colours as Robots in every theme except Classic (rook orange, bishop pink, queen purple, king yellow, knight teal, pawn green): distinct colours help a child tell their pieces apart, so only the costume (a sailor's stripes, a dinosaur's spikes, a space suit's ring) changes, not the body colour. Classic is the one exception throughout, a single natural pair (ivory for the child, black for the other side), matching real chess sets. Footprints follow the same rule: per-type in every theme but Classic, which uses its own single colour. Space adds a static star field and Pirate static wave marks to the background (CSS gradients only, no animation, matching principle 8). Pirate replaces the two edge strips entirely: instead of the eight real-piece silhouettes, each strip is a ship's hull with plank lines and cannon ports, the other side's dark hull above the board and the child's light hull below, so the two sides read as two ships facing off.

The theme picker itself never shows a theme's name or any other text, only each theme's own knight on its own background (a theme's English name exists only as an aria-label, for accessibility and the grown-ups' guide) - the same "no robot word to the child" rule the Robots theme already followed extends to every theme.

**Theme sounds.** Each theme also has its own sound cues (`js/sound.js`, `THEME_SOUNDS`), all synthesized with Web Audio, with no audio files. There is one cue for tapping each piece type (rook, bishop, queen, king, knight, pawn), one for a capture and one for a win. The capture cue rises by one semitone per capture in a row, up to six. Robots: beeps, servo whirrs and a spring boing; Classic: wooden clicks and the plain fanfare; Space: blips, lasers, a thruster whoosh and radar pings; Dinosaurs: roars, stomps, a honk and a chirp; Pirate: coins, a ship's bell, a cannon, a parrot, a whistle and a cutlass. The child's own taps play the piece cue (Home cards, side-panel tiles, and tapping one of their pieces on the board in lessons, capture rounds and games); the app's own demonstrations do not. Every cue is a one-shot under 1.5 seconds and nothing loops.

The turn signals are not themed. The Watch and Your turn sounds, and the smaller sounds (tick, nudge, wrong-tap bonk and the move sounds), are the same in every theme, so the child learns them once (principle on who is in control). A gentle limiter after the master volume keeps overlapping theme sounds from clipping.

## Profiles, stickers and the grown-ups' corner

Stage 5. Progress is kept for each child, on the device only: no login, no cookies, no backend. Everything goes through `js/store.js` (`FC.store`), one JSON document in local storage under the key `footprint-chess`, which is rebuilt field by field from known keys and allowed values on every load and import (see [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md)). If storage is missing, blocked or full, the app keeps working in memory and the grown-ups' corner says progress will not be kept; a document saved by a newer version is never overwritten.

**Children.** Up to four picture profiles: a piece, a theme's artwork and a ring colour (never the Watch purple or the Your turn green), and an optional name of up to 20 characters. A first-time child gets a default profile at once, so there is never a setup screen. With two or more children the app starts on Who's playing (one big picture each, up to three stickers underneath, the optional name small; the line "Who's playing?"); the picture on Home opens it at any time. Choosing a child loads their language, theme and progress. Per child: lessons seen, pieces played, stickers (counted, but never shown as numbers), the capture jar, the team picked per theme, games won per theme, language and theme. A valid `?lang=` or `?theme=` wins at load; the child's own choice is otherwise used.

**Sticker book.** A full-screen book from the button on Home: a tab per theme, two pages of four slots per theme (rook, bishop, queen, king; knight, pawn, golden pawn, golden king). A sticker is a round gold-rimmed badge holding the piece in that theme's artwork on that theme's background; the golden ones are gold with a soft, static glow. Empty slots show a faint outline; stickers earned this visit have a dashed ring; tapping one plays that theme's sound for the piece (the capture sound for the golden ones) without changing the app's theme. In portrait the two pages stack and the slots shrink to fit, so nothing scrolls.

**Grown-ups' corner.** Opened by holding "?" for two seconds (a ring fills; a tap still opens the help page) or from `index.html#grownups`, which the help page links to for keyboard use. It is a modal panel in English (all its text is in one table keyed by language, so it can be translated), with real controls, visible focus, Escape to close and Tab kept inside. Calm mode, the break reminder and its length, the children (picture picker, name, remove with an in-panel confirmation, at most four), a backup code (copy, and paste to restore after an in-panel confirmation; a code that is incomplete, altered, from another version or unreadable is refused with a plain message and nothing changes) and "Clear all progress" (also confirmed).

The last two steps may run longer than the 60 to 90 second rule (owner-approved), with the break reminder still on.

## Check

Stage 7, part A. `js/rules.js` knows check, checkmate and stalemate, and `legalMoves` never lets a move leave the mover's own king in check. The words are "check" and "checkmate" in both languages (Telugu చెక్, చెక్‌మేట్). Only the check games use these rules; every earlier game keeps plain moves, so a king there may still capture a protected pawn.

- **The "Check" lesson** (watch, then do): the child's king on his home row, an opponent rook on his column, the squares between them glowing red: "Check! The rook could capture your king." The king's footprints only show safe squares; he steps out of the line ("Move your king out of danger!", "Your king is safe now!"), then the child does the same. It plays before the child's first game of Get out of check (a game's `lesson` in `js/game-list.js`).
- **Get out of check** (a solo game in a new fourth row of the Games screen, marked with the king in a red ring, beside Keep the king safe, which moved there): five hand-made puzzles, each with the child's king in check and the checking piece glowing red ("Check! Save your king."): two where the king steps away, one where a piece blocks the line, two where the attacker is captured (by a rook, or by the king himself). Puzzles are drawn from a small set and may be mirrored side to side. Only moves that end the check have footprints, so any move solves the puzzle ("Your king is safe now!"); a tap on a square the king may not step to says "Not there! That square is not safe for the king." The idle hint glows the piece that can help. Its "watch how to play" scene shows the three ways in turn: step away, block the line, capture the attacker.
- **The "Checkmate" lesson** (part B): an opponent king stuck behind his own pawns (they glow): "This king is stuck behind his own pawns." The child's rook slides to the far row ("Slide the rook all the way to the other side!") and the king's last squares glow red: "Checkmate! The king has nowhere to go." The child then plays the same move. It plays before the child's first game of Checkmate in one.
- **Checkmate in one** (part B, king row): five hand-made puzzles, easiest first (the rook on the far row, the queen guarded by her king, two rooks, the queen in the corner, a knight against a king boxed in by his own pieces), each possibly mirrored side to side, both kings always on the board ("Find the checkmate!"). Any move that gives checkmate solves the puzzle: the king and every square around him glow red and "Checkmate! The king has nowhere to go." plays. Any other legal move slides back where it came from, and the squares the king could still reach glow red: "Nearly! The king can still escape." The idle hint glows the piece that gives mate, then, once it is selected, its square. The "watch how to play" scene is the lesson's rook mate.

## Mirror Pond: the other side's view

Stage 7. Games about seeing what the other side can do, with the board still never rotating. The hard part for young children is that the other side's forward is toward the child's side: their pawns march down the board and capture on the downward slant. While these games (and their lesson) are on, a still pond fills the far strip, showing the other side's pieces reflected upside down above their own columns, as if seen from their seat; it is decoration only, with no animation, and never shows text. The name "Mirror Pond" is never said to the child; the games have plain names. They sit in a fifth row of the Games screen, marked with a pawn standing over its reflection, together with Run away (which moved there). Rules in `js/pond.js` (tested).

- **The "Their side" lesson** (before the first of these games): the child's pawn marches up ("Your pawns march toward the other side."), the other side's pawn marches down ("Their pawns march toward your side!") and its footprints show in the other side's dark colour, pointing down ("Their footprints point toward your side."). The child then marches their own pawn.
- **Their footprints**: five questions, two pawns, two knights and one other piece, sometimes with one of the child's pieces where it could be captured. The piece glows: "Where can their piece go?" A square it can move to: its dark footprints show and it moves there. Any other square: its footprints show while "Look at how it moves, then try again." plays; after two misses they stay. Idle: the question again, then its footprints.
- **Which piece is in danger?**: five positions, each with three of the child's pieces and one or two of the other side's, exactly one of the child's pieces capturable. "Which of your pieces could they capture?" Another piece gets a green ring: "That one is safe. Try another!" The right one: the attacker's dark footprints reach it and it glows red ("Yes! Now move it somewhere safe."); then its footprints show only safe squares and the rest glow red (tapping one says "Not there! It could capture you there."). Idle: the question again, then the ghost hand on the piece.

## Left and right: the hand-print levels

Stage 7, approved by the owner. The one place where the app names left and right (Telugu ఎడమ, కుడి); everywhere else principle 1 still holds, and `tests/lessons.test.js` checks that only the hand-print lines (`HAND_LINES` in `js/lessons.js`) say either word. Because the board never rotates and the child is always at the bottom, the child's left is always the board's column-0 side. During these levels a left and a right hand print stand at the two ends of the child's own strip, like their own hands on the table: left always orange, right always blue (a left hand seen from the back, thumb toward the middle, and its mirror image). Rules in `js/hands.js` (tested).

- **The "Your hands" lesson** (before the first game): "Hold up your hands and look at them." Each print glows as it is named ("This hand print is your left hand.", "This one is your right hand."), then the rook slides toward the left hand, and the child does the same.
- **Left or right?** (thinking row): six questions, three each way in a random order: a rook, queen or king near the middle, "Move to your left!" with that hand print glowing. Any move that ends further toward the named hand is right ("Yes! That is your left."). A move the other way slides back, the other print glows and "That way is your right. Try your left!" plays; a move straight up or down the board slides back and the question is asked again. Idle: the question again, then the ghost hand on the piece or a square the asked way.

**Run away** (other side's view row, a team game, requested by the owner): the child's piece (the last one chosen on Home) is chased by an opponent piece that moves differently (rook: knight; bishop: rook; queen: knight; king: rook; knight: bishop). Taking turns, the piece may never step where the chaser could capture it: when it is selected those squares glow red, and tapping one says "Not there! It could capture you there." After six safe moves it has got away ("You got away safely!"); capturing the chaser, if it ever allows that, also wins. The chaser never captures, always leaves at least two safe squares when it can, and avoids squares where it could be captured; otherwise it moves to watch as many of the piece's squares as possible. Its "watch how to play" scene shows the knight's footprints, then the rook's safe footprints with the watched squares in red, and the rook stepping to safety.

## The growing battle

Stage 8, approved by the owner, who asked for lessons with more than one piece, adding pieces until the child reaches a full game. The Games screen's sixth row, marked with a whole army (three pawns in front of three pieces), is drawn as a ladder beside the other rows (below them in portrait), the first battle at the bottom. Rules and the opponent in `js/army.js` (tested); `js/games.js` hands every call for these games to it, so the games screen treats them like any other team game (Team card, "Taking turns" lesson the first time, Mission card, "watch how to play" scene, turns, Won card).

- **The battles.** Both sides set up as in a real game, with only that step's kinds of piece. Pawn battle (eight pawns each): the first pawn to reach the other side wins. Pawns and rooks, then pawns, rooks and bishops: capture all their pawns, or reach the other side. Capturing every one of their pawns always wins. No kings yet, so no check; a pawn on the other side simply wins (promotion comes with the full game).
- **Watch how to play.** Each step's scene shows pieces working together: in the pawn battle a pawn protected by another is captured and the other pawn captures back; with rooks, the rook's footprints reach a pawn that is protected (red) and one nobody protects (gold), and the rook captures the free one; with bishops, the bishop is shut in behind its own pawns until a pawn steps forward and it comes out.
- **Help while playing.** The other side's last move (the square it left and the square it reached) glows softly; any of the child's pieces that the other side could capture, with nobody protecting it or by a piece worth less, gets a red ring, and "Careful! One of your pieces could be captured." plays when a piece newly comes into danger. After five seconds of waiting a good piece glows; with a piece selected, its best square glows (same scoring as the opponent's, from the child's side).
- **The gentle opponent.** Not a chess engine: each move is scored (what it captures, whether it walks into danger or saves a piece in danger, bringing pieces out, aiming at something nobody protects, pushing pawns) and the best is played, except that with the step's chance it plays any move that captures nothing instead (45%, 35%, 30%, smaller at each step). It never captures the child below half of their starting pieces or their last piece that is not a pawn, its pawns stop one row short of the child's edge, and it never leaves the child with no move when it has another. If ever nobody can move, the child wins. Tests play many games with a child who follows the hints (every one is won quickly) and with a child who moves at random (every one ends).
