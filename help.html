Footprint Chess

A quick guide for the grown-up in the room.

What it teaches

Footprint Chess teaches how each chess piece moves and captures, using real chess terms (rook, bishop, queen, king, knight, pawn, capture). It never uses the words left, right, or a square name like e4, since those are hard for many young children. Instead, tapping a piece marks every square it can reach with footprints.

The pieces are drawn as robot characters, but this is decoration only: every piece is always named with its real chess name, both in speech and in writing, with a small badge showing the real piece shape.

The flow

Home
Six piece cards. The glowing card is a suggestion, not a requirement: any card can be tapped at any time, and nothing is ever locked.
Meet
The first time a piece is chosen, a card shows the real piece shape next to its robot character and says its name.
Lesson
A short "watch, then do" animation on the board: it plays once by itself, then the child repeats it by tapping the piece and then a footprint. A ghost hand shows where to tap. There is no way to fail; a wrong tap just makes the piece wiggle.
Mission
Before every round, a card states the goal out loud and in pictures: capture all three pawns.
Round
The child moves the chosen piece around the board to capture three opponent pawns. Each capture fills a slot in the side panel.
Won
A short celebration, then a choice: play the same piece again, try the next piece, or go back to the home screen.

Games

Below the six piece cards on the home screen is a second row of three games, each a picture with a short name. All three are played against a gentle opponent: it never plays to win, so the child always finishes, and there are no scores, lives or timers.

Catch the knight
The child chases a knight with the last piece they chose on the home screen (the rook at first). After every move the knight hops away, and after a few moves it gets tired and stops running.
Pawn race
Three pawns against three opponent pawns. The first pawn to reach the other side wins. The opponent never captures the child down to fewer than two pawns.
Little battle
The rook, plus up to two other pieces whose lessons have been seen, clear four opponent pawns. If the opponent captures one of the child's pieces, it comes straight back on the child's side.

The first game played after the page loads starts with a short "Taking turns" lesson (Skip is available), then a card to pick a team. After that a Mission card names the goal, out loud and in a picture, and a tap starts the game. A finished game shows the same kind of card as a round: play again, the next game, or home.

Teams and whose turn it is

Each theme has two teams with real group names: Humanoids and Androids (Robots), White and Black (Classic), Astronauts and Cosmonauts (Space), Theropods and Sauropods (Dinosaurs), and Buccaneers and Corsairs (Pirate). The child picks either team. Whichever they pick always plays from the bottom of the board, since the board never rotates. The first team of each pair moves first, so picking the second team means the opponent makes the first move. In Classic, playing Black gives the child the black pieces.

The two edge strips of the board show the teams during games and the "Taking turns" lesson: the top strip is the other team, the bottom strip is the child's team. The team whose turn it is stays bright and the other dims. This is in addition to the two signals used everywhere else: a purple ring and a "Watch" badge while the opponent (or the app) moves, and a green ring with a "Your turn!" badge, a chime and a spoken "Your turn!" when control comes back to the child. While the opponent moves, the badge names its team ("Androids' turn").

Rewards and breaks

Captures
Each capture gives a small burst and bump, and a chime that rises a little with every capture in a row.
Jar and stickers
Every captured pawn, in games and in capture rounds, fills a jar in the side panel (a picture only, no numbers). When it is full, the child gets a sticker, and the jar empties. Now and then one of the pawns is golden: capturing it gives a sticker at once. Stickers earned so far show as a small row on the home screen. They are kept only until the page is reloaded; nothing is saved on the device.
Break reminder
After about 15 minutes of play (time on the home screen does not count), the next "won" card is replaced by a calm card with a moon, a spoken suggestion to take a little break, a button to keep playing and a button for home. Add ?break=off to the address to turn the reminder off for that visit.

On the board

Watch / Your turn
A purple ring and a "Watch" badge mean the app is showing something; taps on the board do nothing yet. A green ring and a "Your turn!" badge, with a short chime, mean it is the child's turn.
Footprints
Appear on every square the selected piece can move to.
Dashed ring
Marks a square that can be captured this turn, whether it holds an opponent pawn during a round or a lesson's practice target.
Captured pawns
Vanish from the board with a small effect and a sound, and fill a slot in the side panel's goal.
The other side / your side
The board never rotates: the child's side is always the near (bottom) edge, and the opponent's side is always the far (top) edge. These are the only two edges the app ever refers to; it never says left or right.

Buttons

Replay
Plays the current piece's introduction again, starting from the Meet card.
Skip
Shown only during a lesson. Jumps ahead to the Mission card for the piece being taught.
Language
Switches between English and Telugu at any time. The button shows the name of the language it will switch to.
Theme (palette icon, home screen)
Opens a row of five round buttons, one per theme, each showing only that theme's own knight on its own colours (no text, so it never has to name the theme to the child). Tapping one applies it at once. Every theme keeps the same real chess silhouettes, names and badges; only the artwork and colours change.
Sound
Turns sound effects and spoken lines on or off together.
Home
Returns to the home screen and stops whatever is happening.

Language

English is the default. Telugu is available at ?lang=te added to the address, for example https://ireps.github.io/footprint-chess/?lang=te, so it can be bookmarked separately. The language button in the side panel (and on the home screen) switches at any time without losing whatever is currently being said.

Themes

Robots is the default. Classic, Space, Dinosaurs and Pirate are also available, at ?theme=<name> added to the address (for example https://ireps.github.io/footprint-chess/?theme=pirate), so a theme can be bookmarked separately, or from the palette button on the home screen. Every theme keeps the same real chess pieces and names; only the artwork and colours change.

Sound and motion

Spoken lines and sound effects can be turned off with the sound button; both are optional extras, not required to play. All effects are generated in the browser (no audio files), except the lesson voice lines, which use a recorded clip when one exists and otherwise fall back to the device's own speech, or to silence.

If the device's accessibility settings ask for reduced motion, the app shortens its animations automatically. Nothing on screen loops or flashes while the child is deciding what to tap next.

Privacy

Nothing is stored: there is no login, no account, no cookies and no tracking. Progress, including stickers, is not saved between visits. See PRIVACY.md for details.
